MODULE 1

Why Most Implementations Fail Before They Launch

Tool: Rollout Scope & Success Criteria Worksheet

4

Scoping It Like a Real Project

1
Define "done" in behavior, not installation
"Done" means specific people doing specific things differently — not just a login page that works.
2
Name who actually has to change
List the roles whose day-to-day work changes, not just who approved the budget.
3
Set one measurable success signal
Pick a number you'll check 30 days out — usage, not attendance at the kickoff meeting.
5
EXERCISE • 15 MIN

Scope Your Own Rollout

1
Using the Scope & Success Criteria Worksheet, write down what "done" looks like for a rollout you're planning or running.
2
List every role whose daily work actually changes — not just who signed off on the project.
3
Pick the one number you'll check 30 days after launch to know if it worked.

KEY TAKEAWAY

The tool was never the hard part. Getting a busy team to actually change how they work is.

MODULE 2

The Project Plan Nobody Ignores

Tool: Milestone & Owner Tracker

8

Two Kinds of Project Plans

One gets checked every week. The other gets built once and forgotten.

BUILT TO BE FILED
  • Every task the project could possibly touch, in exhaustive detail
  • Lives in a shared drive nobody opens after kickoff
  • No single owner for most milestones
BUILT TO BE CHECKED
  • A short list of milestones that actually matter, each with one owner
  • Reviewed on a standing weekly cadence, not just at kickoff
  • Slipping dates get flagged the week they slip, not the week before launch

The goal isn't a more complete plan. It's a plan short enough that someone actually looks at it.

9

What Makes a Plan Get Used

1
One owner per milestone
Shared ownership quietly becomes no ownership — every milestone needs exactly one name attached.
2
A standing weekly check, not just a kickoff
Five minutes a week catches a slipping date while it's still fixable.
3
Dependencies called out explicitly
The milestone that's actually blocking three others should look visibly more urgent than the rest.
10
EXERCISE • 20 MIN

Build the Tracker

1
Using the Milestone & Owner Tracker, list the 5–8 milestones that actually matter for your rollout.
2

3
Mark which milestones are blocking others, and put those at the top.

KEY TAKEAWAY

A plan nobody checks is just a document that used to be a plan.

MODULE 3

Getting Buy-In Before You Need It

Tool: Stakeholder & Resistance Map

13

Leading With the Why

1
Map who resists and why, before launch
Most resistance is predictable in advance if you actually ask the question.
2
Explain the why before the how
Skip this and people invent their own reason for the change — usually a worse one than the truth.
3
Give real concerns a real channel
A place to raise concerns before launch beats hearing them for the first time during it.
14
EXERCISE • 15 MIN

Map Your Resistance

1
Using the Stakeholder & Resistance Map, list who's affected by your rollout and how likely each is to resist.
2
For your two highest-resistance stakeholders, write the actual "why" you'd give them in one sentence.
3
Decide where people can raise concerns before launch day — not just after.
15

KEY TAKEAWAY

Silence before a rollout usually isn't agreement. It's people who haven't engaged yet.

MODULE 4

Adoption Is the Real Finish Line

Tool: 30-Day Adoption Scorecard

17

Measuring What Actually Happened

1
Track usage, not attendance
Who came to training tells you nothing about who's still using the old spreadsheet three weeks later.
2
Check in at day 30, not just day 1
Old habits creep back quietest in the first month — that's when to catch it.
3
Fix drift while it's still small
A handful of holdouts in week 2 is a conversation. The same holdouts in month three is a culture.
18

Your Action Plan

Before you leave today, answer these:

What does "done" actually mean for the rollout I'm planning or running — and have I written it down?
Who on my team actually has to change their daily work, and have I talked to them yet — not just their manager?
Which milestone in my plan has no single owner right now?
What's the one usage number I'll check 30 days after launch to know if this actually worked?
