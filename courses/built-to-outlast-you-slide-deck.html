Why This Matters

1
Every team has at least one role that would be genuinely hard to backfill quickly — most managers can name it in under ten seconds.
2
Losing that person doesn’t have to mean losing the knowledge. It only does when nothing about it was ever written down.
3
This course gives you a repeatable way to find that risk, document it, and build in enough overlap that no single departure can stall your team.

MODULE 1

What Actually Breaks When Someone Leaves

Tool: Single Point of Failure Map

4

Where the Risk Actually Sits

1
Role concentration
One person holding a task nobody else on the team has ever done from start to finish.
2
Undocumented relationships
Vendor contacts, informal approvals, and "who to call" knowledge that lives in one person's inbox.
3
Tacit know-how
The judgment calls and shortcuts a person has built up over time that were never written down because they didn't feel like "process."
5

The Single Point of Failure Map

1
Using the SPOF Map, list every role on your team and mark who's the sole owner of each critical task.
2
Flag the two or three highest-risk rows — where losing that person tomorrow would genuinely stall the team.
3
Debrief: were any of these risks a surprise, or did you already know exactly where they were?

KEY TAKEAWAY

A team that depends on one person's memory isn't running on a system. It's running on luck.

MODULE 2

Documentation People Actually Use

Tool: SOP Template & Documentation Audit

8

Two Kinds of Documentation

One gets filed away. The other gets opened during a real handoff.

WRITTEN TO CHECK A BOX
  • Long, formal, written to satisfy an audit or a policy requirement
  • Organized the way the writer thinks, not the way a follower would search
  • Nobody has opened it since the day it was filed
WRITTEN TO BE USED
  • Short, scannable, organized around the actual steps someone would take
  • Tested on a person who didn't already know the process
  • Lives where people already look — not a folder three clicks deep

The goal isn't more documentation. It's documentation someone under pressure will actually open.

9

What Makes a SOP Get Opened

1
Keep it short and scannable
If it doesn't fit on one screen without scrolling past the point, most people won't finish reading it.
2
Put it where people already look
A shared drive folder nobody visits is functionally the same as having no documentation at all.
3
Test it on someone who didn't write it
The steps that feel obvious to the author are exactly the ones a new reader gets stuck on.
10
EXERCISE • 20 MIN

Audit One Real Process

1
Pick one process from your team that only one person really knows, and pull up any existing documentation for it.
2
Using the Documentation Audit, score it against the three criteria from the last slide.
3
Identify the single biggest gap — and who could close it this week.
11

KEY TAKEAWAY

Nobody opens a forty-page document during a crisis. Write for the day someone's in a hurry and a little bit scared.

MODULE 3

Cross-Training Without Losing a Quarter

Tool: Cross-Training Coverage Matrix

13

Building Overlap Without Slowing Down

1
Start with the highest-risk tasks
Cross-train the two or three single points of failure first — not everything at once.
2
Shadow before you need it
A planned hour of shadowing costs far less than an emergency scramble after someone's already gone.
3
Rotate on a schedule, not a crisis
A standing rotation keeps a second person current without it ever feeling like an emergency.
14
EXERCISE • 15 MIN

Map Your Coverage Gaps

1
Using the Coverage Matrix, list your team's critical tasks and who the backup owner is for each.
2
Circle every task with no backup listed — that's your real cross-training priority list.
3
Pick one gap to schedule shadowing for in the next two weeks.
15

KEY TAKEAWAY

Cross-training isn't slower work today. It's insurance against a much slower week later.

MODULE 4

The 30-Day Handoff Plan

Tool: 30-Day Handoff Checklist

17

What a Planned Transition Looks Like

1
Weeks 1–2: capture knowledge
Document the process live, while the departing person can still answer questions.
2
Weeks 3–4: shadow & sign off
The incoming person runs the process themselves, with the departing person watching and correcting.
3
Day 30 (and day 60): follow up
Check whether the documentation actually held up under real work, not just on the day it was signed off.
18

Your Action Plan

Before you leave today, answer these:

Which role on my team would be hardest to backfill quickly if that person left tomorrow?
Is there a written SOP for that role's most critical task — and would a new person actually be able to follow it?
Who's the backup owner for my team's two or three highest-risk tasks, and when did they last practice it?
What's the one thing I'll put on this month's calendar to close the biggest gap I found today?
19

The system outlasts the person. That's the whole point.

Document it once. Depend on it every time after.
