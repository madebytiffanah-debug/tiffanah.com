MODULE 1

The Daily Cost Control Cycle

Tool: Par Level & Order Guide Calculator

4

Seven Touchpoints, One Cycle

1
Order & receive
Order to a written par (usage between deliveries, plus a safety factor) — never a guess. Check every delivery line-by-line against the purchase order before signing.
2
Store & issue
FIFO rotation (first in, first out) with everything dated on arrival. Issue controlled quantities, not open access — what's easy to reach gets used, needed or not.
3
Produce, portion & collect
Follow standardized recipes and portion sizes exactly, and reconcile what you produced against what the register shows was sold.

MODULE 2

Contribution Margin vs. Gross Profit %

Tool: Recipe Costing Calculator (with Q-Factor)

8

Two Ways to Look at the Same Plate

They can point in opposite directions — know which one to trust

CONTRIBUTION MARGIN ($)
  • The actual cash left after ingredient cost — price minus cost
  • This is the number that pays rent, payroll, and utilities
  • Best for: deciding which items to push, feature, or cut
GROSS PROFIT (%)
  • Cost as a share of price — can look great on a low-priced item
  • Says nothing about how many actual dollars it produced
  • Best for: overall business efficiency, not single-item decisions

A high-percentage item can lose to a lower-percentage item in real dollars. Menu and pricing decisions should follow the dollar, not the percentage.

9

Costing a Recipe, Step by Step

1
Total ingredient cost
Add up every ingredient at its actual purchase cost for the portion size you serve. This is your raw cost.
2
Add the Q-Factor
Add a small percentage (typically 5–10%) to cover packaging, condiments, and cooking medium that never appear on the recipe card but are still a real cost.
3
Set the price, check both numbers
Divide the Q-Factored cost by your target cost percentage to get a price — then check the resulting contribution margin in dollars before you commit to it.
10

KEY TAKEAWAY

MODULE 3

Waste Elimination & Pre-Shift Lineups

Tool: Pre-Shift Lineup & Waste Audit Builder

13

Catching Cost Before It Repeats

1
Reconcile production to sales
Compare what you prepped against what the register shows was sold. Any gap not explained by logged waste is a discrepancy worth investigating — not adjusting away.
2
Track waste as dollars, not a shrug
Log every spoiled, dropped, or comped item with a dollar value attached. A pattern you can't see in ounces becomes obvious in dollars.
3
Run a 5-to-10-minute pre-shift lineup
Set one or two specific cost priorities right before service starts — a portioning reminder, a waste target, a par to watch — while it's most likely to change behavior.
14

KEY TAKEAWAY

Your Action Plan

Before your next shift, answer these:

What are my written par levels for the three items I order most often — and are they actually written down anywhere?
Which item on my menu or package list looks efficient by percentage but is actually a weak dollar contributor?
Am I logging waste as a dollar figure, or just tossing it and moving on?
What's the one specific priority I'll lead with at my next pre-shift lineup?
