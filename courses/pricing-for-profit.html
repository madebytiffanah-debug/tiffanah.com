Same loss, two different controls problems

Same missing $400. One of these setups catches it fast. Module 2 covers why.

Growth doesn't fix weak pricing or weak controls — it just makes the losses bigger.

A price set by gut feel might survive at one location with an owner watching every till. It rarely survives at three locations with a management layer in between. The same is true for controls: informal, trust-based processes that worked at a small scale become the exact gap that larger operations lose money through.

This course gives you the math to set a price with a break-even behind it, the controls that catch a problem in days instead of months, and a way to measure the health of your operation beyond just this month's revenue number.

Who this is for

General managers and multi-unit managers responsible for pricing decisions, financial controls, and overall operational health — especially those scaling from a single location or department into managing several at once.

Runs best when…

  • Participants bring a real pricing decision or control gap from their own operation
  • Groups stay between 10 and 18 given the density of the controls material
  • Each person leaves with a completed self-audit checklist for their own unit
  • Findings from the self-audit are reviewed with ownership or finance within 30 days

Try a sample question

One question from the course quiz — see how it feels before you dive in.

One employee opens the register, rings sales, processes refunds, and reconciles the drawer at close — all without anyone else reviewing it. What control principle does this violate?

Segregation of duties means no single person should control an entire transaction from start to finish — ringing the sale, processing a refund, and reconciling the cash. When one person does all three, an error or theft has no natural checkpoint where it would get caught.
