Same performance data, two framings

Same facts, different frame. Module 2 teaches managers to separate the data from the narrative.

Bias rarely looks like prejudice. It looks like a confident, well-intentioned snap judgment.

Mental shortcuts, first impressions, and in-group signals shape hiring, review, and promotion decisions long before a manager consciously feels like they've decided anything. Trying harder to "be objective" in the moment doesn't fix this — understanding the mechanics and structuring the process does.

This workshop replaces guesswork with the specific, learnable cognitive patterns behind biased decisions, and the structural fixes that actually work.

Who this is for

People managers involved in hiring, performance calibration, or promotion decisions. The exercises use realistic candidate and employee profiles, so the workshop works best scheduled for an intact cohort.

Runs best when…

  • Participants are at the same management level, so decision-sharing stays candid
  • Each person brings one recent hiring or calibration decision from their own experience
  • Groups stay between 12 and 24 for full small-group participation
  • A 30-day manager check-in follows to reinforce the material

Why do structured interviews reduce bias compared to unstructured conversation?

Structured interviews use pre-set questions and scoring criteria asked identically to every candidate, which measurably reduces the influence of first-impression halo effects compared to unstructured conversation.
