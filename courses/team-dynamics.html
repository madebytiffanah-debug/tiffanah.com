Managers run meetings every week. Most were never taught how groups actually behave.

A bigger meeting or a longer discussion doesn't automatically produce a better decision. Effort quietly drops when contributions aren't identifiable, unique information gets buried under shared talking points, and group discussion tends to push decisions toward extremes rather than moderate them.

This workshop replaces guesswork with the specific, learnable mechanics behind group decisions, leadership fit, and fair feedback.

Who this is for

People managers and team leads who run meetings, make group decisions, or evaluate team performance. The exercises use live, in-room decision simulations, so the workshop works best scheduled for an intact cohort.

Runs best when…

  • Participants are at the same management level, so decision-sharing stays candid
  • Each person brings one recent team decision or feedback moment from their own team
  • Groups stay between 12 and 24 for full small-group participation
  • A 30-day manager check-in follows to reinforce the material

Try a sample question

One question from the course quiz — see how it feels before you dive in.

According to group polarization research, what tends to happen after a group discusses a decision?

Group polarization means discussion strengthens the group's average initial leaning rather than moderating it — a group already leaning cautious becomes more cautious, one leaning risky becomes riskier.
