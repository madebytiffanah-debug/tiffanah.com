Every department shows a profit. Did the night make money?

Same six departments, same numbers. Module 1 covers the line that changes the answer.

Six departments can each look healthy and the show can still lose money.

F&B, Talent, VIP and bottle service, merch, ticketing, and sponsorships each run their own numbers — but none of them include security, cleaning, or utilities. Those sit below every department as shared overhead, and they're exactly the line that turns a profitable-looking lineup of departments into a losing night.

This course teaches you to consolidate six streams into one statement, understand how a talent settlement actually reconciles after a show closes, and recognize when a stream is deliberately run at breakeven to drive money somewhere else.

Who this is for

The capstone in this series — built for anyone who now needs to explain a consolidated, multi-stream P&L to ownership, not just their own department's numbers. Courses 1–3 are not required, but the material assumes comfort with Prime Cost, contribution margin, and reading a departmental statement.

Runs best when…

  • Participants bring an actual multi-stream night or event to work through together
  • Groups stay small, between 8 and 15, given how specific the settlement math gets
  • At least one person present has actually closed out a real artist settlement
  • Finance is available afterward to reconcile the workshop numbers against real reports

Try a sample question

One question from the course quiz — see how it feels before you dive in.

An artist's contract includes a $600 hospitality rider budget, agreed at time of booking. Actual hospitality spend for the show comes to $850. What typically happens to the $250 overage?

Hospitality and tech riders are each budgeted separately at time of booking, but actual spend on each is only known after the show. When actual spend on either one exceeds its own budgeted amount, that overage is typically backed out at settlement — reconciled against what's owed to the artist — rather than silently absorbed or ignored.
