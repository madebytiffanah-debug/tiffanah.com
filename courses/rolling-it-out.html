Same rollout, two different launch days

Same system, same launch date. The difference is everything that happened in the weeks before it. Module 2 covers how.

A system going live isn't the finish line. People actually using it is.

Most rollouts get treated like an IT event — pick the tool, schedule the migration, send an announcement. But the tool was never the hard part. The hard part is getting a team that's already busy to change how they work, and that takes the same discipline as any other project: a real scope, a plan people actually follow, and buy-in secured before launch day instead of scrambled together after it.

This workshop replaces "we bought the software, so now what" with a repeatable way to scope a rollout, build a plan that doesn't rot in a shared drive, and measure whether it actually worked by usage — not just whether it launched on schedule.

Core takeaway, Module 1

Who this is for

Department heads, project leads, and managers rolling out a new tool, process, or system to their team — especially useful before a rollout is already scheduled, while there's still time to scope it properly.

Runs best when…

  • Participants bring a real upcoming or recent rollout from their own team
  • Each person leaves with a scoped plan and a stakeholder map for their own rollout
  • Groups stay between 10 and 20 for hands-on template work
  • A 30-day follow-up checks adoption numbers, not just whether the system went live

Try a sample question

One question from the course quiz — see how it feels before you dive in.

Your team is three weeks from launching a new scheduling system. Adoption has been quiet — no complaints, but nobody's said anything positive either. What's the highest-leverage thing to check before launch day?

Silence before a rollout usually isn't agreement — it's people who haven't engaged yet. Checking whether they understand the "why" and have had a real chance to raise concerns catches resistance before launch day, instead of during it.
