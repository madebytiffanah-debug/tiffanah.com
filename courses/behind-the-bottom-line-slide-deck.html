Why This Matters

1
You cannot manage a financial statement — you can only manage the operation that produces it. The P&L is a mirror, not a cause.
2
A department head who reads a P&L as a report card misses the point. The real skill is tracing every variance back to the decision that caused it.
3
This course teaches you to read the statement, diagnose why a number moved, and see how it all connects to EBITDA and company value.

MODULE 1

Reading a Departmental P&L

Tool: Prime Cost Benchmark Tracker

4

Reading Down the Statement

1
Revenue
The top line. Every other line on the page is measured as a percentage of this number, so it's the first thing to understand, not just the first thing to read.
2
Prime Cost (COGS + Labor)
The two costs a department head actually controls day to day through ordering, scheduling, and portioning. This is the number to watch above all others.
3
Gross Operating Profit (GOP)
What's left after operating expenses, before fixed costs like rent, insurance, and corporate overhead. The cleanest read on how well the department itself is run.
5
EXERCISE • 15 MIN

The Prime Cost Benchmark Drill

1
Using the Prime Cost Benchmark Tracker, enter last month's revenue, COGS, and labor for your department.
2
Compare your Prime Cost % to the target range for your type of operation.
3
Discuss: if Prime Cost is above target, which side moved — COGS or labor — and why?
6

KEY TAKEAWAY

Revenue tells you how business was. Prime Cost tells you how well it was run. Watch the second one at least as closely as the first.

MODULE 2

Cause-and-Effect Variance Analysis

Tool: P&L Variance Diagnostic

8

Which Story Is True?

The same revenue change can mean two very different things

VOLUME STORY
  • Covers or traffic actually changed
  • Shows up as revenue and volume moving the same direction
  • Fix: marketing, hours, capacity, demand generation
RATE STORY
  • Price or sales mix changed, not traffic
  • Can mask a real decline in volume underneath it
  • Fix: menu engineering, pricing, upsell training

You cannot tell which story is true from the top-line number alone — you have to break volume and rate apart first.

9

Diagnosing a Variance, Step by Step

1
Isolate volume
Multiply last period's rate by this period's volume. That isolates how much of the change is due to volume alone, holding price constant.
2
Isolate rate
Multiply this period's volume by the change in rate. That isolates how much is due to price or mix alone, holding volume constant.
3
Check that they add up
Volume effect plus rate effect should equal the total variance. If covers fell but revenue rose, the rate effect is doing all the work — and may be masking a traffic problem.
10
EXERCISE • 20 MIN

Run the Diagnostic

1
Use the P&L Variance Diagnostic with your own last-two-periods numbers.
2
Identify whether your variance is volume-driven, rate-driven, or a mix of both.
3
Debrief: does the cause match what you assumed before running the numbers?
11

KEY TAKEAWAY

A rising number isn't automatically good news, and a falling one isn't automatically bad. Break it into volume and rate before you decide which.

MODULE 3

EBITDA & the Secret Link to the Balance Sheet

Tool: EBITDA Builder & Balance Sheet Link

13

From Revenue to EBITDA, and Beyond

1
Start at Gross Operating Profit
Subtract the fixed costs not yet accounted for — rent, insurance, corporate overhead — to get to operating income.
2
Add back interest, taxes, depreciation & amortization
That's EBITDA — a clean read on operating performance regardless of how the company is financed or taxed, which is exactly why it's the benchmark investors use.
3
Follow profit to the balance sheet
Net profit for the period doesn't just disappear — it flows into Retained Earnings under Equity, literally becoming part of what the company is worth.
14
EXERCISE • 15 MIN

Build It Yourself

1
Use the EBITDA Builder to walk from your department's revenue down to EBITDA.
2
Watch how the number changes as interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization are added back.
3
Discuss: why might two departments with identical net profit have very different EBITDA?
15

KEY TAKEAWAY

EBITDA is the business's engine, measured without the noise of financing or taxes. It's also next month's starting equity, one retained-earnings entry at a time.

Your Action Plan

Before your next department meeting, answer these:

Do I know my department's actual Prime Cost % from last month, or am I estimating?
The last time a number moved, did I check volume vs. rate before reacting to it?
Could I explain what EBITDA measures to someone with zero finance background?
What's one number on my P&L I've never actually investigated the cause of?
