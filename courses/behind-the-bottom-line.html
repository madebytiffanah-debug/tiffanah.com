Same 8% headline, two completely different operational stories. Module 2 covers how to tell them apart.

Operations is the cause. The P&L is just the mirror.

You cannot manage a financial statement — you can only manage the operation that produces it. A department head who reads a P&L as a report card misses the point; the real skill is tracing every variance back to the shift-level decision that caused it.

This course teaches you to read down a departmental statement to Gross Operating Profit, diagnose why a number moved instead of just noticing that it did, and understand the one number — EBITDA — that ties your operating decisions to the company's actual value.

Who this is for

Department heads and assistant general managers who now see a full P&L for their area but haven't been formally taught how to read one — especially those coming up from a Course 1–style operational role into their first numbers-facing position.

Runs best when…

  • Participants bring last month's actual departmental P&L, redacted if needed
  • Groups stay between 10 and 20 so real statements can be discussed candidly
  • Each person leaves having diagnosed one real variance from their own numbers
  • A finance partner is available afterward for follow-up questions

Try a sample question

One question from the course quiz — see how it feels before you dive in.

Last month's revenue was $80,000 from 4,000 covers. This month's revenue is $86,400 from 3,600 covers. What actually happened?

Average check went from $20.00 ($80,000 / 4,000) to $24.00 ($86,400 / 3,600) — a 20% increase that more than made up for a 10% drop in covers. A rising revenue number hid a falling traffic count. That's exactly the kind of thing volume-vs-rate analysis catches and a top-line glance misses.
