Break-Even, Broken Down

1
Fixed vs. variable costs
Fixed costs (space rental, dedicated staff base pay, equipment) don't change with volume. Variable costs (per-guest food, per-unit supplies) scale directly with it. Mixing the two up is the most common pricing mistake.
2
The break-even formula
Break-even volume = Fixed Costs ÷ (Price − Variable Cost per Unit). Below that volume, you're losing money on the package no matter how good it looks on paper.
3
Tiered pricing captures more
Offering Bronze / Silver / Gold versions of the same package lets price-sensitive and value-seeking customers both say yes, instead of one price under-serving both.

Internal Controls That Actually Prevent Loss

Tool: Internal Controls Self-Audit Checklist

8

Which Setup Catches the Problem?

Same missing $400 — two very different outcomes

DUTIES SEPARATED
  • Different people ring sales, approve refunds, and reconcile the drawer
  • A missing $400 shows up on this week's audit log
  • Errors and theft get caught in days, not months
ONE PERSON, ALL DUTIES
  • The same employee controls the entire cash cycle
  • A missing $400 has no natural checkpoint to get caught at
  • Problems can run for months before anyone notices

Segregation of duties isn't about distrust — it's about making sure a problem has somewhere to get caught.

9

Three Controls Habits

1
Reconcile the stock ledger
Compare what your inventory records say against what the general ledger reflects, on a set schedule — not only when something already feels wrong.
2
Keep accounts payable current
An aging AP report slipping past 30, 60, or 90 days is often the first visible sign of a cash flow problem, not a vendor problem.
3
Audit POS voids and comps
Review void, refund, and comp reports regularly, watching for patterns tied to a specific employee, shift, or time of day.

The Balanced Scorecard

Tool: Balanced Scorecard Builder

13

Four Quadrants, One Scorecard

1
Financial
Revenue, Prime Cost, net profit margin — the numbers most managers already track, and the last ones to show a problem.
2
Customer & Process
Net Promoter Score and guest satisfaction on one side; inventory turnover and throughput speed on the other — how the operation runs, and how it feels to the people it serves.
3
People
Employee engagement and retention — often the earliest warning sign of trouble, showing up here months before it ever shows up in revenue.
14
EXERCISE • 15 MIN

1

2
Identify which quadrant you track the least closely today.
3
Discuss: has a people or process metric ever predicted a financial problem before it showed up in revenue?
15

KEY TAKEAWAY

Revenue tells you about last month. Your people and process metrics tell you about next month. Track both.

Your Action Plan

Before your next planning cycle, answer these:

Can I calculate the break-even volume for my three biggest packages or offers right now?
Is there any single person in my operation who controls an entire transaction from start to finish?
When did I last reconcile my stock ledger against the general ledger?
What's one non-financial metric I should be watching more closely than I currently am?
17

Price with math. Control with structure.

Growth doesn't fix either one — it just raises the stakes.
